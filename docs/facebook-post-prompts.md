# Facebook Post Prompts — Paul Learning Center

Ten standalone prompts for generating promotional posts (image + caption).
Each block is self-contained: copy one whole prompt into your generator and
it carries its own facts, brand spec, and guardrails. Nothing here needs the
other nine to make sense.

Every number and name below comes from PLC's own published results flyers and
public Facebook reviews. **Do not invent, round up, or embellish any of it.**

---

## Before you start — two practical notes

**1. AI image generators render text badly.** For the achievement posts
(1, 2, 3, 8, 9, 10) the text *is* the design, so build those as graphics in
Canva/Figma using the layout spec given — don't ask an AI to render the
numbers and names. For the photo-led posts (4–7) the AI prompt generates the
*photograph*; the text goes on top afterwards.

**2. Never caption a stock or AI photo as "our students."** PLC's real
students appear only on the published flyers. A generated classroom photo is
illustration, not documentation — treat it as such, or use a real PLC photo.

## Universal guardrails — include these in every generation

Do **not**:

- Guarantee or imply a grade, score, or admission outcome ("we'll get your child an A", "guaranteed improvement").
- Use superlatives PLC cannot prove: *best*, *#1*, *top-rated*, *leading*, *guaranteed*, *fastest*.
- Invent statistics, student counts, years in business, pass rates, or testimonials.
- State prices, class sizes, schedules, or session dates — **none are confirmed yet**.
- Imply affiliation with or endorsement by any school district, college, or the College Board.
- Generate images depicting identifiable children presented as PLC's real students.
- Alter a review's wording, or present a parent's opinion as PLC's own claim.

Do:

- Attribute every named achievement to the semester it came from.
- Frame benefits as *what we do*, not *what your child will get*.
- Keep captions under ~120 words, lead with the point, one clear CTA.
- Use 3–6 relevant hashtags maximum.

# POST 1 — The headline number

**Goal:** Open the campaign with the single most credible aggregate fact.
**Format:** 1080 × 1080.

**IMAGE — build as a graphic (do not AI-render the text):**

> Deep teal `#0B5F54` to teal `#14A08D` diagonal gradient background.

**CAPTION — write in this direction:**

performer. Close by pointing to the results page so parents can check the
names themselves. Tone: quietly confident, verifiable, not boastful.
CTA: "See every name → paullearningcenter.com/results/"

**Facts you may use:** 13 perfect scores; 3 published semesters; results span
Math 1 through AP Calculus and ELA.
**Do not:** claim a percentage of students who achieve this, or compare PLC
to other centers.

# POST 5 — How the teaching actually works

**Goal:** Set honest expectations — the method, not a promise.
**Format:** 1080 × 1080.

**IMAGE — AI photo prompt, text overlaid afterwards:**

> "A close, warm photograph of a tutor's hands and a student's hands over a
> notebook on a wooden table, working through a handwritten math problem
> together; a pencil mid-explanation, two different attempted approaches
> visible on the page; soft natural window light from the left, shallow depth
> of field, muted natural colors, documentary style, no faces visible, no text
> or signage in frame, 1:1."
>
> Overlay: bottom third, white-to-transparent scrim, heading in ink —
> **"Explained as many ways as it takes."**

**CAPTION — write in this direction:**
Describe the approach in three concrete beats: work stays aligned to what the
student faces in class and on exams; a concept gets re-explained a different
way rather than repeated louder; the student does the work while the tutor
checks understanding. Then the honest part — say plainly that progress depends
on the student's starting point and effort, and that PLC will tell a family
straightforwardly what to expect rather than promise a grade.
CTA: tell us your child's grade and where they're stuck.
Tone: practical, no salesmanship.

**Do not:** promise timelines ("results in 4 weeks"), guarantee grade
improvement, or describe class sizes or session length.
